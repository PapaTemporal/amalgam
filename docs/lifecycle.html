What happens, and when

A session with amalgam has three moments that do real work: the one where it opens, every one where you ask something, and the one where it ends. Almost all of it happens on your machine. These diagrams show which parts, and what crosses the wire when they don't.

The whole shape

The line across the middle is the only thing you pay for. Read the diagram by asking how often anything crosses it: twice per question, and never at all when a session ends.

1

The session opens by reconciling

A session ends by writing down what it learned and rebuilding what fell behind. That covers everything that session did, and nothing that happened between sessions — a pull, a branch switch, work on another machine, an editor open in another window.

Those land on the state an agent trusts most. A stale index does not answer more slowly; it answers wrongly, in the same tone as a right answer. So the opening asks two questions of reality rather than of a cache.

It reports; it does not repair. Rebuilding here would either make you wait for your first prompt or move the index underneath a session already using it.

2

A question becomes evidence, not files

This is the loop that runs all day. The claim it rests on: the graph decides which lines matter, and the working tree supplies what they say. Those are two different sources, and keeping them separate is what makes a stale graph cost precision instead of correctness.

3

The session ends without making you wait

A hook runs while you are waiting for your prompt back, so the rule is strict: the cheap half happens now, the expensive halves are handed to detached children and forgotten about. Nothing here can delay a session ending, and nothing it fails at can prevent one.

Two ways into memory, one of them gated

Four layers, and two ways into the one that matters. Telling the agent something is worth keeping writes it there and then. What a finished session merely inferred is different: nothing it guessed becomes a memory without a person saying so. Recall treats every stored fact as equally true, which is exactly why the second door has a queue in front of it.

Choosing what answers you

The newest piece, and off by default. A session otherwise runs on whatever model the agent defaults to, which means the same model answers "what does this function return" and "redesign the scheduler". Sizing a task is a classification, and that is the thing a small local model is genuinely good at.
